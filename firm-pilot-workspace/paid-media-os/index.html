When I walk in

  1. 1Get approved access (Ads, CallRail, CRM, GTM/GA4, pages).
  2. 2Baseline ~10 accounts — Google metrics and business outcomes, separately.
  3. 3Prove whether Google is learning truth or garbage.
  4. 4Fix conversion signals before campaign cosmetics.
  5. 5Pilot, measure, then automate only proven audits.

Central thesis

Biggest risk is often conversion signal quality, not campaign structure. Legal: expensive clicks, low volume, noisy leads. A call or form is not automatically valuable.

junk / spam wrong practice wrong geo existing client vendor / job prospect qualified consult retained

Loop (conceptual)

Click → paid LP → GTM/GA4 → CallRail/form → CRM → AI + human review → CRM outranks guess → qualified signal back to Google.

Hard rule

AI must not invent giant conversion values because a transcript “sounds good.”

Conversion auditdeep

Inspect Primary/Secondary actions, GA4 imports, CallRail, forms, click-to-call vs connected, duplicates, micro-conversions training Smart Bidding, double-counting, campaign goals vs defaults.

Signal depth — deeper verified outcome = stronger signal. Use the deepest event with enough volume.

  1. 1Raw lead / raw call
  2. 2AI-qualified (confidence + human review)
  3. 3Actual qualified lead
  4. 4Consultation
  5. 5Retained case
  6. 6Expected value → realized fee
Do not delete old conversions immediately. Preserve history while validating replacements.
CallRail / call quality

Audit DNI, pools, source, recording, transcript, duration, answer/missed, geo, tags, API/webhooks, GCLID linkage, CRM reach.

AI classification uses transcript content, not duration alone:

  • Valid prospect? Relevant practice / geo?
  • Legal intent? Already represented? Viable?
  • Consultation-worthy? Obviously junk?
  • Confidence — humans review uncertain; sample high-confidence early
CRM outcomes should outrank AI guesses. Duration is a filter, not qualification.
CRM audit

CRM(s), stages, update quality, GCLID/source/campaign, CallRail fields, value fields, API, offline upload reliability.

Standard funnel (map client words into this):

  1. 1Lead
  2. 2Qualified lead
  3. 3Consultation
  4. 4Retained case
Different CRMs are fine. Unnormalized reporting across 100 firms is not.
GTM audit

Ownership, duplicates, unused tags, custom HTML, triggers, Google tag, GA4, Ads, CallRail, forms, consent, click-ID capture.

Don’t rebuild WordPress analytics by default. A paid microsite can have clean tracking while the main site stays messy.
GA4 audit

Correct property, streams, Ads links, key events, duplicates, custom dims, referrals, forms, call events, paid LP measurement.

Don’t automatically create a new GA4 property if useful history exists. New stream only when justified.
Landing pages / microsites

Speed, mobile, message match, practice/geo specificity, CTA, sticky phone, form friction, trust, disclaimers, CallRail swap, click IDs, CRM routing.

First-stage pattern: one client → one Cursor project → one repo → one paid host. Templates later — don’t mash firms into one mega-project on day one.

Campaign audit

Structure, budget fragmentation, bidding, match types, negatives, search terms, RSAs, LP mapping, IS, qualified CPL, cost/retained.

Ugly naming ≠ bad performance. Don’t burn campaigns just because you dislike inherited systems.

Low-budget legal ($1.5K–$5K)signal volume

Small budgets can’t support many campaigns and experiments. Signal density is scarce.

  • Consolidate; highest-intent first
  • Clean signals; realistic geography
  • Highest-quality conversion that still has volume
  • Don’t optimize only to retained if too few
  • AI-qualified conversations can be intermediate; still feed retained downstream
$2,000 across 10 campaigns teaches Google almost nothing useful.
Structure rules

Do not standardize campaign counts. Standardize decision rules.

  • Brand Search — separates SEO/GBP demand; stops brand masking weak nonbrand
  • Consolidated Nonbrand — split only when economics/geo/budget earn it

Few tightly themed ad groups by true case category — not dozens of campaigns.

New practice area ≠ automatic new campaign. Same budget → something else loses allocation.
Match types & negatives
  • Start: Core Exact + Core Phrase; highest-intent commercial first
  • No 80 permutations — modern exact/phrase already expand
  • Broad / AI Max earned by signal quality — not Google’s recommendation alone

Three negative classes:

  1. 1Universal junk — jobs, salary, law school, wrong practice/geo
  2. 2Routing — brand vs nonbrand; categories only when volume earns it
  3. 3Learned — terms that repeatedly fail downstream
Don’t auto-negative informational PI language (insurance, settlement). Let outcomes decide.
Competitors & DKI
Block hard
Pure navigation (“phone number,” “address”) on mega-brands — budget sinkholes
Keep / classify
Local/mid competitor + case intent; remembered name as marketplace breadcrumb
At $20–30K
Separate conquest often not justified — classify inside consolidated structure first

DKI inserts the matched keyword from the account — not the user query. Only in tightly controlled ad groups. Never in competitor/catch-all/broad sets.

Geography & creative
  • “Near me” only works when the attorney is perceived as near the searcher
  • Don’t manufacture city creative just because targeting includes a city
  • Service area ≠ equally valuable geography — track retained by geo

At legal CPCs: one control LP (maybe one challenger); one hypothesis at a time. RSAs with real messaging differences.
