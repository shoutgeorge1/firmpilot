FirmPilot Paid Media Operating System — v2

Practical cockpit for onboarding, first-10 audits, conversion repair, and turning proven PPC logic into AI-assisted portfolio ops across ~100 legal clients. Living draft — refine from real account data. Do not invent FirmPilot production facts.

Meeting guide (talk tracks) Jump to First 10 Things I still need
Day-one question: What exactly do I do when I walk in? Get access → baseline 10 accounts → prove whether Google is learning truth or garbage → fix signals before structure theater → pilot, measure, then automate repetitive audits.

Central thesis — conversion signal quality

The biggest issue may not be campaign structure. It may be conversion signal quality. Legal accounts often have expensive clicks, low volume, small budgets, and noisy leads.

A phone call or form is not automatically a valuable conversion. Distinguish:

  • junk / spam · wrong practice · wrong geo · existing client · vendor / job seeker
  • legitimate prospect · qualified lead · consultation · retained / signed · high-value matter

Long-term loop: Ads click → paid LP/microsite → GTM/GA4 → CallRail or form → CRM → AI classifies with confidence → human reviews uncertainty → CRM outcome outranks AI guess → qualified conversion / value back to Google → then broaden matching and bidding.

AI must not invent giant conversion values because a transcript “sounds good.”

First 10 account pilot

01 Pilot purpose & workflowdo this first

Use 10 representative accounts before touching all ~100. Prove the operating system on a tractable set: budgets that vary, at least one messy conversion setup, at least one stronger account if available.

  1. Access & ownership map (company vs client systems)
  2. Baseline Google metrics and business outcomes — separately
  3. Conversion / CallRail / CRM / GTM / GA4 / LP / campaign audits
  4. Recurring failure patterns across the ten
  5. One clean signal framework + limited structural fixes
  6. Compare before/after → expand only what worked
A portfolio of 100 without a proven pilot just multiplies garbage at scale.

Entry / access phase

02 Systems, ownership, permissions

For each system: who owns it, company vs client, read vs write, and how I get in.

Never store credentials in this local page. Map owners and ask for approved access paths.

Questions for the AI engineering team

03 What exists before I invent anything
  • What have you already automated?
  • What exactly do you mean internally by a “skill”?
  • What APIs are already connected?
  • Do you already have Google Ads API access? Developer-token access level?
  • How do you separate clients / tenants?
  • What CRM(s)? Same CRM for most firms? How is client/tenant/location identified?
  • Are CallRail calls available via API/webhook? Transcripts?
  • Are lead statuses standardized?
  • Is anything already sending downstream conversions back to Google?
  • How are secrets stored? What gets logged?
  • Are AI / agent sessions retained internally?
  • What can existing agents safely WRITE vs only READ?
AI should industrialize proven PPC logic — not invent PPC strategy blindly.

Baseline audit (before major changes)

04 Google metrics vs business outcomes

Google performance metrics

  • Spend · clicks · CPC · CTR
  • Ads conversions · reported CPA · CVR
  • Budget · bidding strategy · campaign types
  • Keyword / match structure · search terms
  • IS · lost IS budget · lost IS rank
  • Geo · device · day/hour
  • Landing pages · conversion actions

Actual business outcomes

  • CallRail calls · form leads
  • Qualified leads (if known)
  • Consultations
  • Retained / signed cases
  • Qualified lead CPA
  • Retained case CPA
Never conflate Google “conversions” with retained cases. Capture both sides before changing the account.

Conversion audit

05 Integrity of every conversion actiondeep

Inspect Primary and Secondary actions, GA4 imports, native Ads, CallRail, forms, click-to-call vs connected calls, duplicates, obsolete actions, micro-conversions used for bidding, page views / button clicks as “business outcomes,” double-counting, attribution, campaign goals vs account defaults, and whether Smart Bidding trains on garbage.

Signal ladder (conceptual):

  1. Raw lead / raw call
  2. AI-qualified lead (confidence + human review)
  3. Actual qualified lead
  4. Consultation
  5. Retained case
  6. Expected case value → realized fee

Deeper verified outcome = stronger signal. Find the deepest event that still has enough volume to optimize against.

TRUST KEEP BUT MODIFY MOVE TO SECONDARY REPLACE INVESTIGATE
Do not delete old conversions immediately. Preserve history while validating cleaner replacements.

CallRail / call quality

06 Tracking + AI classification concept

Audit: DNI, pools, source attribution, recording, transcription, duration, answer rate, missed calls, caller geo, tags/dispositions, API/webhooks, GCLID / landing / campaign linkage, CRM reach.

AI call classification evaluates transcript content, not duration alone:

  • Valid prospective client? Relevant practice? Relevant geo?
  • Legal intent? Already represented? Viable matter?
  • Consultation-worthy? Obviously junk?
  • Confidence score — humans review uncertain; initially sample high-confidence too
CRM outcomes should eventually outrank AI guesses. Duration is a filter, not qualification.

CRM audit

07 Pipeline truth + FirmPilot standard funnel

Inspect CRM(s), whether they differ by client, stages, stage consistency, intake update quality, GCLID / source / campaign storage, phone/email, CallRail fields, qualified / consult / retained, case type, value fields, API/webhooks, history, offline upload reliability.

FirmPilot standard funnel (map client words into this):

  1. Lead
  2. Qualified Lead
  3. Consultation
  4. Retained Case
Different CRMs are fine. Unnormalized reporting across 100 firms is not.

GTM audit

08 Containers, tags, paid isolation

Ownership, tag count, duplicates, unused, custom HTML, naming, triggers, variables, Google tag, GA4, Ads conversions, CallRail, forms, phone, consent, click-ID capture, CRM handoff.

KEEP EXISTING GTM CLEAN EXISTING GTM CREATE CLEAN PPC GTM FOR NEW PAID ENV
Do not rebuild the whole WordPress analytics world by default. A clean paid microsite can have clean PPC tracking while the main site stays messy.

GA4 audit

09 Property, events, Ads link

Correct property, streams, Ads links, key events, duplicates, custom dims/metrics, referrals, forms, call-related events, paid LP measurement.

Do not automatically create a new GA4 property if useful history already exists. New stream only when justified.

Landing page / microsite audit

10 Control the paid funnel

Destinations, speed, mobile, message match, KW→ad→page, practice/geo specificity, headline, CTA, phone/sticky call, form friction, credibility, testimonials, results (if appropriate), trust, disclaimers, length, CVR, CallRail swap, GTM/GA4, click IDs, CRM routing.

KEEP CURRENT PAGE IMPROVE CURRENT PAGE BUILD NEW PAID PAGE BUILD NEW PAID MICROSITE

First-stage build pattern: one client → one Cursor project → one GitHub repo → one Vercel (or host) paid deploy. Reusable templates later — don’t mash all firms into one mega-project on day one.

Campaign audit

11 Structure vs performance honesty

Structure, budget fragmentation, practice/geo splits, types, bidding, match types, KW duplication, negatives/lists, search terms, RSAs/assets, LP mapping, device/schedule/geo, goals, IS / lost IS, CPC, CVR, qualified lead rate, cost/qualified, cost/retained.

KEEP CLEAN UP CONSOLIDATE REBUILD TEST AGAINST NEW STRUCTURE
Ugly naming ≠ bad performance. Preserve useful history. Don’t burn campaigns solely because you dislike inherited systems.

Low-budget legal accounts ($1.5K–$5K)

12 Signal-volume reality

Small budgets cannot support many campaigns, practice splits, and experiments. Signal density is the scarce resource.

  • Consolidate; highest-intent traffic first
  • Clean conversion signals; realistic geography
  • Avoid fragmentation
  • Use the highest-quality conversion that still has enough volume
  • Do not optimize only to retained cases if there are too few
  • AI-qualified conversations can be an intermediate bidding signal
  • Still feed retained outcomes downstream for calibration
A $2,000 budget across 10 campaigns teaches Google almost nothing useful.

Structure rules (starter architecture)

13 Brand + consolidated nonbrand$20–30K PI

Do not standardize campaign counts. Standardize decision rules.

Typical starting shape for a mid legal budget:

  • A. Brand Search — separates demand FirmPilot SEO/GBP may create; stops brand from masking weak nonbrand
  • B. Consolidated Nonbrand Acquisition — split only when economics/geo/budget earn it

Within nonbrand: few tightly themed ad groups by true case category — not dozens of campaigns.

New practice area does not automatically earn a campaign. Same budget → something else loses allocation.

Match types & negatives

14 Controlled keywords + negative taxonomy
  • Start: Core Exact + Core Phrase; highest-intent commercial terms first
  • No 80 permutations — modern exact/phrase already expand
  • Broad / AI Max earned by signal quality — not Google’s recommendation alone

Three negative classes:

  1. Universal junk — jobs, salary, law school, wrong practice/geo, unusable intent
  2. Routing — brand vs nonbrand; categories routed only when volume earns separation
  3. Learned performance — terms that repeatedly fail downstream qualification
Do not auto-negative informational PI language (insurance, settlement, rear-ended). Let call/intake outcomes decide.

Competitors & DKI

15 Decision trees
Block hard
Pure navigation (“phone number,” “address”) on mega-brands — budget sinkholes
Keep / classify
Local/mid competitor + case intent; remembered name as marketplace breadcrumb
At $20–30K
Separate conquest often not justified — classify inside consolidated structure first

DKI inserts the matched keyword from the account — not the user query. Only in tightly controlled ad groups where every keyword is safe as copy. Never in competitor/catch-all/broad exploration sets. Manual creative for intentional conquest.

Geography & creative

16 Near-me honesty · RSA testing
  • “Near me” only works when the attorney is perceived as near the searcher
  • Don’t manufacture city creative just because targeting includes a city
  • Service area ≠ equally valuable geography — track retained outcomes by geo

At legal CPCs: one control LP (maybe one challenger); one meaningful hypothesis at a time. RSAs with real messaging differences — not 15 “Call Today” clones. DKI is one RSA component, not the whole strategy.

Bidding / expansion roadmap

17 Stronger expansion after stronger signal
Phase 1

Measurement clean

  • Tracking verified
  • Structure sane
  • High intent captured
Phase 2

Qualified signal reliable

  • Smart Bidding gets better inputs
  • Manual / Max Clicks only as baseline
Phase 3

Broader matching

  • Test broad / AI Max where appropriate
  • Watch junk-call burden
Phase 4

Value optimization

  • Downstream value when data supports it
  • No invented transcript values
Phase 5

Other inventory

  • PMax / other only when it solves a real problem
Stronger targeting expansion comes after stronger signal. Do not change five major variables at once if you need to know what caused the result.

Channel expansion order (none automatic): broader Search → AI Max tests → Search Partners → competitor conquest → Microsoft → other Google → Meta/video → other. Search gets money first if it can consume budget efficiently.

Client governance: committed categories / geo / budget / expected acquisition range for a period. New category → more budget or something else loses allocation.

AI / automation

18 Assist → skills → portfolio

Initially: AI assists me — reads exports, audits, patterns, search terms, transcripts, recommendations. I review.

Then: repetitive audit logic becomes automation / skills, e.g.:

  • account health · conversion integrity · search terms · negatives
  • budget pacing · lead quality · CallRail · landing pages
  • tracking integrity · CRM outcomes · new-account setup · weekly PPC review
Do not automate a decision until I’ve done it manually enough times to know the rules. AI eng industrializes proven logic.

Scale needs reliable data, QA, API capacity, and manageable exceptions — not merely more tokens. People still own client expectations, judgment, policy exceptions, and hard measurement.
